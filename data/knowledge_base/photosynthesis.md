# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars. In plants it takes place in chloroplasts, organelles that contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

## Overall equation
The net reaction is: 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6 O2. Carbon dioxide and water are consumed; glucose and oxygen are produced.

## Light-dependent reactions
These reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. Chlorophyll absorbs mainly red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green. The absorbed energy splits water molecules (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a by-product, and drives the production of the energy carriers ATP and NADPH.

## Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)
The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast. Using the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, the enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto ribulose bisphosphate. The cycle produces glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), which the plant uses to build glucose and other carbohydrates.

## Adaptations
C4 plants such as maize and sugarcane separate carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle in different cell types to reduce photorespiration in hot climates. CAM plants such as cacti open their stomata at night to save water.
