# The Eiffel Tower

## Construction and purpose
The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. It was built as the entrance arch to the 1889 World's Fair (Exposition Universelle), which marked the centennial of the French Revolution. Construction began on 28 January 1887 and finished on 31 March 1889, taking just over two years.

The tower is named after the engineering company of Gustave Eiffel, which designed and built it. The initial design was produced by engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, and the architect Stephen Sauvestre refined its appearance. Gustave Eiffel himself did not draw the original design; his company carried it out.

## Materials and dimensions
The structure is made of puddled (wrought) iron, not steel. It consists of 18,038 individual iron parts held together by about 2.5 million rivets. The ironwork weighs roughly 7,300 tonnes and the whole structure about 10,100 tonnes.

The tower is 330 metres (1,083 ft) tall including its antennas. When it was completed it stood 312 metres tall. It was the tallest man-made structure in the world until the Chrysler Building in New York City was completed in 1930.

## Temporary permit and later use
The tower was not meant to be permanent. The original permit allowed it to stand for only 20 years, after which it was to be dismantled. It was saved because of its value as a radiotelegraphy station; antennas on the tower were used for wireless telegraphy experiments and military communication.

## Maintenance and visitors
The tower is repainted about every seven years to protect the iron from rust. It is one of the most visited paid monuments in the world, with several million visitors each year.
